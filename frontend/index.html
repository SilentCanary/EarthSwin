How it works

From two satellites to one warning

Every 1.28 km square of terrain goes through the same four steps. Clouds, missing radar dates and steep shadows are handled inside the model, not patched around it.

1

See

Six optical bands from before and after the monsoon, plus fifteen radar dates at 12-day spacing. Radar keeps working under cloud.

2

Understand the slope

Eight terrain channels and rainfall totals feed a factor-of-safety estimate, so the model knows which slopes can physically fail.

3

Compare neighbours

Graph attention lets each square look at the eight nearest ones, and temporal attention reads the radar dates that actually exist.

4

Report

A calibrated probability, an honest uncertainty, outlined landslide objects of at least 900 m², and a warning level with a review flag.

Where the project stands

Built and tested. Waiting for real labels.

Everything below the data line is verified on synthetic terrain. No accuracy figure for Himachal exists yet, and this page will not show one until there is one.

  1. All 15 blocks of the diagram, 52 tests passingEncoder, fusion, graph, temporal, physics, evidential decoder, calibration, explainability, serving.
  2. Baselines and honest evaluationSlope, NDVI, radar and gradient-boosting baselines, plus U-Net, siamese and U-TAE networks. Block-bootstrap intervals, ablation runner, ensembles.
  3. Real-data toolingEarth Engine export, chip builder with layover masking, chip QA with a label-alignment test.
  4. Needed: 2023 landslide polygonsMandi and Kullu inventory from IIT Mandi / Durham, and the statewide HPSCCC inventory. Nothing can be trained on Himachal without them.
  5. Next: one-tile pilot, then full export and trainingChip QA decides whether the labels line up with the imagery before any long run.
  6. Phase 2 early warning, Phase 3 self-trainingStart after the detector works on real terrain.

Why Himachal

The 2023 monsoon in numbers

Published figures that frame the problem. They are context for the project, not outputs of the model.

~6,500

landslides mapped from the 2023 monsoon, across about 3,100 km² of Mandi and Kullu. Sana et al.

509

deaths reported by the state disaster authority by the end of August 2023, with at least 2,200 homes destroyed. Down To Earth

Mandi · Kullu · Shimla

the districts with the worst damage in the July 2023 event. Sana et al., Landslides

10 m

pixel size PMGST-Net works at, so a single scar of about 900 m² (nine pixels) can be outlined.

Data

What goes in

All imagery and terrain come through Google Earth Engine on a fixed UTM grid of 10.24 km tiles, each with a 2.56 km buffer so context never stops at a tile edge.

InputSourceWhat the model gets
OpticalCOPERNICUS/S2_SR_HARMONIZEDB2, B3, B4, B8, B11, B12 composites before (May–Jun) and after (mid-Sep to Oct) the monsoon, with a cloud mask
RadarCOPERNICUS/S1_GRDVV and VH medians in fifteen 12-day bins from 1 May; no-data and layover stay masked
TerrainCOPERNICUS/DEM/GLO30Elevation, slope, aspect, plan and profile curvature, wetness index, relief
RainfallUCSB-CHG/CHIRPS/DAILYMax 1-day, max 3-day, 7-day and 30-day totals before the post image
GroundESA WorldCover, OpenLandMap soil, GSI geology when availableWater mask and a rock or soil class per pixel
LabelsIIT Mandi / Durham 2023 inventory, HPSCCC inventoryLandslide polygons; unmapped ground is ignored, never counted as "no landslide"
